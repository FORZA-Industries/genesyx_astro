## What we claim, and what we don't

**We do:** coordinate the parts of the Shettles approach (nutrition, a supplement formula, at-home
testing and timing) into one clear, calm programme, and tell you what the research actually says.

**We don't:** claim that any product, food, test or timing can guarantee, or reliably change, the sex of a
baby. If anyone tells you otherwise, ask to see the evidence.

## The research at a glance

| Question | What the best evidence says | Strength of evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does timing intercourse change the baby's sex? | Two large prospective studies found no link | Fairly strong: consistent, prospective, hundreds of pregnancies |
| Do Y sperm swim faster, or X sperm live longer? | Modern studies find no meaningful difference | Moderate: lab studies using reliable genetic identification |
| Does vaginal pH favour X or Y sperm? | No human evidence that it does | Weak: theory only |
| Does diet change the odds? | One small study reported an effect; a larger link was later questioned | Weak: small, not replicated |

## Timing of intercourse

The central idea of the Shettles method is that when you have sex relative to ovulation changes the odds of
a girl or a boy. This has been tested directly.

- **Wilcox, Weinberg and Baird (1995)** followed 221 women trying to conceive, measuring hormones daily to
  pin down ovulation. Across 625 cycles and 192 pregnancies, cycles that produced boys and girls had
  similar patterns of intercourse. The authors concluded that, for practical purposes, timing has no
  influence on the sex of the baby.
- **Gray and colleagues (1998)** analysed 947 births to women using natural family planning charts. The sex
  ratio did not vary consistently or significantly with the timing of intercourse relative to ovulation.
- **France and colleagues (1984)**, a smaller study of 33 pregnancies, found the opposite trend to Shettles:
  if anything, more boys were conceived when intercourse was several days before ovulation. The authors
  said the result refuted the idea that sex close to ovulation favours boys.

## Do X and Y sperm behave differently?

Shettles based his theory on the belief that Y-bearing sperm are smaller and faster, and X-bearing sperm are
larger and hardier. Later research using reliable genetic identification has not supported this.

- **Hossain and colleagues (2001)** compared X and Y sperm identified by fluorescence in situ hybridisation
  and found no significant differences in head size, shape or tail length.
- **A 2019 review by Rahman and Pang** concluded that recent studies show negligible or no difference between
  X and Y sperm in shape, size, motility, swimming pattern or pH response. The main established difference
  is the amount of DNA they carry.

## Vaginal pH

A healthy vagina is naturally acidic. The Shettles theory proposes that a more acidic environment favours
girls and a more alkaline one favours boys, but we are not aware of any good-quality human study showing
this. We include pH tests so you can see your readings, not because pH has been shown to change the odds.
More in our [vaginal pH guide](/vaginal-ph).

## Diet

- **Mathews, Johnson and Neil (2008)** reported that women with the highest energy intake around conception
  were more likely to have boys (56% versus 45%).
- **Young, Bang and Oktay (2009)** re-examined the analysis and argued the result was most likely a false
  positive caused by testing many foods at once.
- **Noorlander and colleagues (2010)** reported more girls among a small group of women who combined a low
  sodium, high calcium diet with timing intercourse well before ovulation. The group that met the study's
  criteria was 32 women, and the finding has not been independently repeated.

## How to read this

Taken together, the research does not support the claim that natural methods reliably change a baby's sex.
Slightly more boys than girls are born naturally, roughly 105 for every 100 girls, and that baseline is the
most honest expectation for any couple.

Genesyx exists for couples who want to try the Shettles approach anyway and would rather do it with good
information, safe products and a clear plan than with guesswork.
