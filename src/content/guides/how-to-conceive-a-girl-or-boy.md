## The honest starting point

Slightly more boys than girls are born worldwide, roughly 105 boys for every 100 girls. The sex of a baby
is decided by whether the sperm that fertilises the egg carries an X or a Y chromosome, and no natural
method has been shown to reliably change that.

## The two routes at a glance

| | Hoping for a girl (XX) | Hoping for a boy (XY) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shettles timing | Intercourse 2–4 days before ovulation | Intercourse as close to ovulation as possible |
| Vaginal environment (theory) | More acidic | More alkaline |
| Reasoning in the theory | X-bearing sperm are said to survive longer | Y-bearing sperm are said to swim faster |

## Timing

Every timing-based method depends on knowing when you ovulate. Our guide to
[ovulation tests and your fertile window](/ovulation-tests) explains how to find it. Timing intercourse away
from ovulation, as the girl route suggests, may make conception take longer.

## Vaginal pH

The Shettles theory proposes that vaginal pH may affect X- and Y-bearing sperm differently. Genesyx
includes at-home [vaginal pH tests](/vaginal-ph) so you have readings rather than guesses. **Never douche or
use home remedies** to change your pH; they can cause irritation and infection.

## What the research says

A widely cited 1995 study in the *New England Journal of Medicine* found no link between the timing of
intercourse relative to ovulation and the sex of the baby. More on our [evidence page](/evidence).

## Things to keep in mind

- If you have been trying to conceive for over a year (or six months if you are over 35), speak to your GP.
- In the UK, choosing a baby's sex through IVF for non-medical reasons is not permitted.
