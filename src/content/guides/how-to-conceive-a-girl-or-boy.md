## The honest starting point

A baby's sex is set at fertilisation, by whether the sperm that reaches the egg carries an X chromosome
(a girl) or a Y chromosome (a boy). Slightly more boys than girls are born naturally, roughly 105 boys for
every 100 girls. No natural method has been shown to reliably change that.

That doesn't stop many couples from trying, and it is worth knowing what each approach involves and what the
research says before you start.

## The two routes at a glance

| | Hoping for a girl (XX) | Hoping for a boy (XY) |

| Reasoning in the theory | X-bearing sperm are said to survive longer | Y-bearing sperm are said to swim faster |

## Method 1: timing intercourse

Timing is the heart of the [Shettles method](/shettles-method), and every timing plan depends on knowing
when you ovulate. Our guide to [ovulation tests and your fertile window](/ovulation-tests) explains how to
find it.

**What the research says:** two large prospective studies, one in 1995 following 221 women with daily
hormone tests and one in 1998 covering 947 births, found no link between when couples had sex relative to
ovulation and the sex of the baby. A smaller 1984 study found, if anything, the opposite trend to the one
Shettles predicted.

## Method 2: vaginal pH

The Shettles theory proposes that a more acidic vagina favours girls and a more alkaline one favours boys.
Modern studies have found no meaningful difference between X and Y sperm in how they respond to pH, and there
is no good-quality human evidence that pH changes a baby's sex.

Genesyx includes at-home [vaginal pH tests](/vaginal-ph) so you have readings rather than guesses. **Never
douche or use vinegar, baking soda or other home remedies** to change your pH; the NHS advises against
douching because it can cause infection.

## Method 3: diet

Some studies have linked what a woman eats around conception with her baby's sex:

- A 2008 UK study of 740 women reported more boys among those with the highest energy intake. Other
  researchers later argued this was most likely a statistical false positive.
- A small 2010 Dutch study reported more girls when a low-sodium, high-calcium diet was combined with timing
  sex well before ovulation. It involved only a few dozen women and has not been repeated.

Neither finding is strong enough to rely on. Whatever route you choose, eating a varied, balanced diet
before pregnancy matters far more for you and your future baby.

## Positions, orgasm and other folklore

Advice about sexual positions, orgasm, the Chinese birth calendar and similar ideas is widely shared online.
None of it has good evidence behind it.

## Trying safely

- **Take folic acid** while trying to conceive: the NHS recommends 400 micrograms a day.
- **Don't let the method delay pregnancy** longer than you are comfortable with, especially on the girl
  route, which avoids the most fertile days.
- **See your GP** if you have been trying for a year without success, or sooner if you are 36 or over.
- In the UK, selecting a baby's sex through fertility treatment is only allowed to avoid a serious
  inherited condition. It is not permitted for personal preference.

## What the research says, in one line

The evidence does not support any natural method of choosing a baby's sex. Genesyx is for couples who want
to try the Shettles approach anyway and would rather do it with accurate information, safe products and a
clear plan. The full list of studies is on our [evidence page](/evidence).
