## What is the fertile window?

An egg lives for about 12 to 24 hours after it is released. Sperm can survive inside the body for several
days (the NHS says up to seven). That combination creates a short fertile window each cycle.

A landmark 1995 study that tracked ovulation with daily hormone tests found that pregnancies came only from
sex during a **six-day window ending on the day of ovulation**. The chance of conceiving rose from about 10%
when sex was five days before ovulation to about 33% on the day of ovulation itself.

## How ovulation tests work

Ovulation tests measure **luteinising hormone (LH)** in your urine. LH rises sharply shortly before an egg
is released; this is the LH surge. Ovulation usually follows within about **one to two days** of the surge
starting, so a positive test means your most fertile days are now.

A few tips for clearer results:

- Test at the same time each day, usually late morning or early afternoon
- Avoid drinking a lot in the two hours before testing, as it dilutes your urine
- Follow the timing on your test's instructions exactly
- Keep testing until you get a positive, then stop for that cycle

## When to start testing

Ovulation usually happens about **10 to 16 days before your next period**, not a set number of days after
your last one. Around 14 days before is typical, so the longer your cycle, the later you ovulate. Start
testing a few days before you expect to ovulate.

| Cycle length | Likely ovulation day | Start testing around |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 24 days | Day 10 | Day 7 |
| 26 days | Day 12 | Day 9 |
| 28 days | Day 14 | Day 11 |
| 30 days | Day 16 | Day 13 |
| 32 days | Day 18 | Day 15 |
| 35 days | Day 21 | Day 18 |

These are estimates. If your cycles vary, base your start day on your shortest recent cycle.

## Other signs of ovulation

- **Cervical mucus** becomes clearer, wetter and stretchier, like raw egg white, around ovulation.
- **Basal body temperature** rises slightly _after_ ovulation, so it confirms ovulation has happened rather
  than predicting it.

## Timing and the Shettles routes

The Shettles method uses ovulation timing in opposite ways for its two routes: sex two to four days before
ovulation when hoping for a girl, and as close to ovulation as possible when hoping for a boy. Research has
not found that either route changes the baby's sex. See how the routes compare in our guide to
[conceiving a girl or boy](/how-to-conceive-a-girl-or-boy).

One practical point: the girl route asks you to stop before your most fertile days, so conception may take
longer. If getting pregnant matters more to you than the route, the NHS advice is regular sex every two to
three days throughout your cycle, which means you do not need to pinpoint ovulation at all.

## When to get advice

Speak to your GP if:

- your cycles are very irregular, or your tests never show a surge
- you have been trying to conceive for a year without success
- you are 36 or over, in which case the NHS suggests seeing your GP sooner
