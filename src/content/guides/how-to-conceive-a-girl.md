## The honest starting point

Slightly more boys than girls are born worldwide, roughly 105 boys for every 100 girls. Whatever approach
you follow, the sex of your baby is decided by whether the sperm that fertilises the egg carries an X or a
Y chromosome, and no natural method has been shown to reliably change that.

## Methods couples commonly try

### 1. Shettles timing

The Shettles method suggests having intercourse **two to four days before ovulation** and avoiding it
closer to ovulation. The theory is that X-bearing sperm survive longer. Read the full
[Shettles method guide](/shettles-method).

### 2. Vaginal environment

The theory also proposes that a more acidic vaginal environment may favour X-bearing sperm. Genesyx uses
at-home [vaginal pH tests](/vaginal-ph) so you have readings, not guesses. **Never douche or use home
remedies** to change your pH.

### 3. Diet

Some small studies have linked diet around conception with a baby's sex, but the findings have not been
reliably replicated. The Genesyx XX nutrition guide focuses on a balanced preconception diet.

## Things to keep in mind

- Timing intercourse away from ovulation may make conception take longer.
- If you have been trying for over a year (or six months if you are over 35), speak to your GP.
- In the UK, choosing a baby's sex through IVF for non-medical reasons is not permitted.
