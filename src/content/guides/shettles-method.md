## Where the theory comes from

Dr Landrum B. Shettles was an American obstetrician and fertility researcher. In the 1960s he proposed that
sperm carrying a Y chromosome (which leads to a boy) are smaller and faster, while sperm carrying an X
chromosome (which leads to a girl) are larger, slower and longer-lived. From that idea he developed a set of
steps for couples hoping for one or the other.

His method reached a wide audience through the 1970 book _Your Baby's Sex: Now You Can Choose_, written with
the journalist David Rorvik, and later through updated editions titled _How to Choose the Sex of Your Baby_.

## What the method proposes

| | Trying for a girl (XX) | Trying for a boy (XY) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Intercourse 2–4 days before ovulation, then stop | Intercourse as close to ovulation as possible |
| Vaginal environment | More acidic | More alkaline |
| Reasoning in the theory | X-bearing sperm survive longer, so they outlast Y sperm | Y-bearing sperm swim faster, so they reach the egg first |

The original book also suggested vinegar or baking-soda douches to change vaginal pH. **Genesyx does not
recommend this.** The NHS advises against douching because it can upset the healthy balance of bacteria and
make infection more likely.

## What the research says

The method's core idea has been tested, and the results do not support it.

- **Timing:** a 1995 study in the _New England Journal of Medicine_ followed 221 women with daily hormone
  measurements and found that cycles producing boys and girls had similar patterns of intercourse. A 1998
  study of 947 births reached the same conclusion.
- **Sperm differences:** modern studies that identify X and Y sperm genetically have found no meaningful
  difference in size, shape or swimming speed.
- **pH:** there is no good-quality human evidence that vaginal pH favours X or Y sperm.

The full list of studies, with links, is on our [evidence page](/evidence).

## So why follow it?

Some couples want to try anyway, and many already do, often with guesswork, unsafe home remedies and
conflicting advice. The method's timing steps are harmless in themselves. The trade-off is that the girl
route asks you to stop having sex before your most fertile days, which can mean it takes longer to conceive.

If you choose to try, it is better to do it with accurate ovulation tracking, safe products and a clear
view of what the evidence does and doesn't say.

## A note on safety

- Never douche or use vinegar, baking soda or other home remedies to change your vaginal pH.
- Take folic acid while trying to conceive: the NHS recommends 400 micrograms a day.
- If you have been trying for a year without success, or sooner if you are 36 or over, speak to your GP.
