## Where the theory comes from

Dr Landrum B. Shettles was an American physician and fertility researcher. In the 1960s he proposed that
sperm carrying an X chromosome (which leads to a girl) and sperm carrying a Y chromosome (which leads to a
boy) behave differently. His ideas were popularised in the book *How to Choose the Sex of Your Baby*, first
published in 1970.

## What the method proposes

| | Trying for a girl (XX) | Trying for a boy (XY) |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Intercourse 2–4 days before ovulation | Intercourse as close to ovulation as possible |
| Vaginal environment | Theory suggests a more acidic environment | Theory suggests a more alkaline environment |
| Rationale in the theory | X-bearing sperm are said to survive longer | Y-bearing sperm are said to swim faster |

## What the evidence says

Later studies have not consistently supported the theory. A widely cited 1995 study in the *New England
Journal of Medicine* found no link between the timing of intercourse relative to ovulation and the sex of
the baby. You can read more on our [evidence page](/evidence).

## A note on safety

Never douche or use home remedies to change your vaginal pH. They can disrupt healthy bacteria and cause
infection. If you have any concerns, speak to your GP or a pharmacist.
